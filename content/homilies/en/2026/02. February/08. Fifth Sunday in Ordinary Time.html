Fifth Sunday in Ordinary Time

Jesus says to us: “You are the light of the world.” Christianity is not a path of private holiness, closed in on my personal relationship with God. Jesus came into the world to save all people, and therefore He calls His disciples to proclaim the Good News to everyone.

Everyone who receives this Good News also takes on the responsibility of passing it on to others. But not because we want to increase the number of Christians, or because God needs more followers. Jesus says clearly: “Let your light shine before others, that they may see your good works and give glory to your Father in heaven.”

A Christian, then, is called to be light for others, showing that God is our Father who loves us. And this is done not primarily through words, but through deeds — deeds of love and mercy.

In the first reading, the prophet Isaiah tells us that good deeds heal our wounds. Jesus goes even further and teaches us that by helping others, we help ourselves first. Works of mercy are not about earning a reward in heaven; they are already living the Good News here and now.

This is what Christians are meant to proclaim. This is what they are meant to witness to: a love that makes helping others a joy rather than a burden. We are called to witness that the poorest of the poor, if he shares his last piece of bread with another, is far richer than the greatest wealthy person whose selfishness closes his heart to the joy that comes from giving.

This is something Saint Francis of Assisi taught us so clearly — the joy of being poor yet feeling rich, because one knows how to share.
