First Sunday of Lent

Lent is a time of conversion — a time to acknowledge sin and to take up the struggle against it. That is why, on the First Sunday of Lent, the Church invites us to deepen our understanding of what sin truly is. Very often our understanding of sin is limited to condemning its most extreme forms, such as murder, theft, or adultery, while we forget about its roots.

That is why, in the first reading, we heard about original sin. It is not only the first sin in human history, but also the model of every sin. Every sin contains elements of original sin. Above all, we see that sin is always connected with the temptation of Satan. If we truly want to fight sin, we must first try to understand the mechanism of that temptation.

Satan does not always tempt us to do something that is obviously evil. Very often he tempts us by presenting something that appears good, while at the same time suggesting that the way to achieve that good requires “going around” God’s commandment.

Today’s Gospel shows us how subtle and refined such temptations can be. We usually assume that Satan would never tempt us to pray, to trust God, or to rely on Him. And yet, these are precisely the most dangerous temptations for believers.

The First Temptation — Bread Before God

The first temptation Satan presents to Jesus concerns prayer — “Give us this day our daily bread.” Satan says: if you believe that God is your Father who loves you, then He cannot allow you to starve or for anything bad to happen to you. Just ask, and God will surely give you what you want.

We might ask: where is the temptation here? Didn’t Jesus Himself teach us to ask our heavenly Father for everything we need? The temptation lies in reversing the order. In the Lord’s Prayer, we begin with: “Thy will be done”, and only then do we say: “Give us this day our daily bread.” Satan wants us to start with bread and forget about God’s will.

The Second Temptation — Testing God

The second temptation is even more subtle — the temptation of “trusting God.” This is the temptation of people who sincerely want to live by faith. Satan whispers: “If you trust God, you don’t need to worry about anything. God will take care of you and your family. You are sick — trust God. Someone in your family is sick — place it in God’s hands. God is the best doctor.”

And again, what seems wrong with that? Jesus answers clearly: “You shall not put the Lord your God to the test.”

God does not ask us to close our eyes to reality or to jump into the abyss blindfolded. He calls us to use the means available to us — for example, to go to a doctor. God works primarily through ordinary means, not through miracles performed on demand simply because we want them.

The Third Temptation — Doing Good the Wrong Way

The third temptation is the temptation to do good. I want to do something good — for the Church, for my country, for my family, for the poor. But I lack money, resources, influence. And once again Satan “comes to help.” He says: be realistic. If you want money, if you want support from powerful people, sometimes you have to make compromises. A small compliment to a politician, turning a blind eye to where the money comes from — it’s such a small thing. And think of all the good you will be able to do with those resources.

Once again, Jesus responds: “You shall worship the Lord your God, and Him alone shall you serve.”

True good comes only through obedience to God’s commandments, not through bypassing them — even if our motives appear noble and sincere.

Conclusion

At the beginning of Lent, it is worth becoming aware of these strategies of satanic temptation. Very often we are deeply convinced that we are acting “in the name of God,” while in reality we are acting in the name of Satan. Satan has no hesitation in using words such as: “pray,” “trust God,” or “say the rosary,” as tools of temptation.

Lent is not only a time of beating our breasts and saying, “I am a sinner.” It is above all a time to understand what sin truly is and to seek help from Jesus Christ — the only One who can set us free from the slavery of sin.
