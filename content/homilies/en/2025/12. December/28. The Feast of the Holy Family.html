The Feast of the Holy Family

Today’s feast shows us how important the family truly is. The Savior does not come to us directly, but through a family. It is within a family that He is not only born into the world, but — as the Gospel tells us — He grows and becomes strong, filled with wisdom. God Himself grows and gains strength within a human family.

This message seems especially relevant today, when fewer and fewer children are able to grow up in a family where both a father and a mother are present. A teacher once told me that in her class of twenty or thirty students, only one child lives with both his father and mother. Life takes many different paths, and this is not about criticizing anyone. Rather, it is about recognizing in the Holy Family an ideal worth striving for — an ideal of a father and a mother caring for their child together.

What do we see when we look at the Nativity scene? Extreme poverty, and at the same time, great happiness. The Holy Family shows us that when love is present in a family, material conditions become secondary. The presence of a father in the home means more than the money he might earn.

When communism ended in my country and the borders opened, many men left to work in Germany, France, and other European countries where wages were much higher. Indeed, children from those families were often better dressed and could afford fashionable gadgets. Many teenagers even said that it was better that their father was not at home — fewer demands and more freedom.

But as those children grew older, problems began to appear: alcohol, drugs, trouble with the law. Long-term absence of the father also led to many marriages breaking apart. I mention this so that when we look at the Nativity scene and at the Holy Family, we do not only feel sentimental, but also learn and take them as an example.

The first lesson the Holy Family teaches us is that it is worth sacrificing many things — good jobs, money, careers — so that the family can be together. The presence of parents in a child’s life is irreplaceable.

The second lesson from today’s Gospel concerns order within the family. Looking at the Holy Family, we can see two kinds of order. The first is the order of importance: at the center is the Child Jesus, then His Mother Mary, and somewhat in the background, Saint Joseph. But there is also another order — the order of responsibility and obedience. Here the order is reversed: Joseph is the head of the family, the one who makes decisions and takes responsibility for its future; then Mary; and finally Jesus, who — as we read — was obedient to His parents.
