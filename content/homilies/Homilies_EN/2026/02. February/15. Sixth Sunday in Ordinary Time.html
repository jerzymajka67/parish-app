Sixth Sunday in Ordinary Time

Saint Paul speaks to us today about wisdom — but not human wisdom, rather the wisdom that comes from God.

Human wisdom is necessary, for example, to build a car, a computer, or other tools. But that is not enough. We also need another kind of wisdom — the wisdom that teaches us how to use these things properly.

God created the human person so that, in His name, humanity might rule over creation. Yet when humanity turned away from God, it not only lost the wisdom needed to use created things for its own good, but very often became a slave of creation — and frequently a slave of what it had created itself.

If we lack true wisdom, the wisdom that comes from God, we may soon discover that we have become slaves even to artificial intelligence, which we ourselves have created. For true wisdom is the ability to distinguish good from evil.

The first reading teaches us something very important. We often think that life is about choosing between mixtures of good and evil: a little good mixed with a little evil on one side, and something similar on the other. And then we struggle with what to choose.

We sometimes hear opinions such as: to reach heaven, one must suffer on earth. Or the opposite: we can have a happy life here on earth, but then we will not receive eternal life.

Sirach tells us clearly: on one side of the scale there is good, and on the other there is evil. Good is what God offers us. Evil is what Satan proposes.

God offers us a happy life on earth and eternal life with Him in heaven. Satan wants us to be unhappy here on earth — and unhappy for eternity. Jesus comes to open our eyes to this deception, to this lie of the evil one.

At the same time, Jesus warns us: “To whom much is given, much will be required.”

The more we understand what sin is, the greater our responsibility when we commit it ourselves or allow others to commit it. If I unknowingly give someone something that harms them, my guilt is much less than if I do so knowingly.

That is why the righteousness of a Christian, enlightened by the light of the Holy Spirit, must be greater than that of the Pharisees and the scribes.

Let us pray, then, that we may not fall into the traps of Satan, but that we may always choose the good. A good that has no shades and is not relative, but the true Good — the good that is good for everyone: faithfulness to God’s commandments.
