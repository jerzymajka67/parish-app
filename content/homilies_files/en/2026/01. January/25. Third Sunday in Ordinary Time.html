Third Sunday in Ordinary Time

The Evangelist Matthew interprets Jesus’ return to Galilee as the fulfillment of Isaiah’s prophecy: “The land of Zebulun and the land of Naphtali, Galilee of the Gentiles; the people who walked in darkness have seen a great light; and for those who dwelt in the land of the shadow of death, a light has risen.”

Today these words sound poetic to us, but in the eighth century before Christ they were not poetry. The people of Galilee truly lived “in the shadow of death,” and that shadow came from Assyria.

King Sennacherib of Assyria invaded the lands known in Jesus’ time as Samaria and Galilee, then part of the Kingdom of Israel. Many were killed, and the rest were taken into exile. From that time we speak of the ten lost tribes of Israel.

Isaiah proclaims that liberation will come for those living under this power of evil. Yet centuries passed and the exiles did not return. And here Matthew invites us to read this history differently.

In a sense, we are all those ten lost tribes. We all live in the shadow of death — not the shadow of earthly empires, but the shadow whose source is sin.

Jesus comes to free us from this slavery.

We often imagine salvation as the transformation of the world around us, liberation from external powers of evil. But Jesus says something radically different: the source of evil is sin, and the battle begins not with changing the world, but with personal conversion.

“Repent,” Jesus says — turn away from sin, so that we may receive the joy of the Good News that the Kingdom of God is near.

The Kingdom of God is near because it is already within us. When we turn away from sin and begin to live according to God’s will, the Kingdom of God has already begun in us. As Saint Paul says, the Kingdom of God is not about food and drink, not about comfort and prosperity, but about righteousness, peace, and joy in the Holy Spirit.

By turning away from sin and learning to live in love, we allow the light of Christ to scatter the shadow of death within us, because wherever we begin to love, the Kingdom of God is already in our midst.
