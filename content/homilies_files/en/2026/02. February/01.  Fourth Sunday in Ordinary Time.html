Fourth Sunday in Ordinary Time

We have grown accustomed to hearing the so-called Beatitudes. We know them well, have heard them many times, and so they no longer shock us. Yet they should.

The Messiah came so that there would no longer be the poor, the suffering, and the persecuted; to establish such a peace that those who work for it would no longer be needed. Yet Jesus says something quite the opposite: the more there are poor, suffering, and persecuted people, the more God’s blessing is revealed on earth.

To understand Jesus’ words properly, it is helpful to read them in the light of what Saint Paul tells us today. The Apostle reminds us that God has chosen what is foolish in the eyes of the world, what is weak and despised, to shame the wise and powerful. He does this so that the world may see that true wisdom and strength come from God, not from human beings.

Man often thinks he is self-sufficient. He thinks he knows how the world should be; that if he only had enough strength, power, and might, he could make the world better than God created it. Yet the more we rely on our own wisdom and power, trying to “improve” God, the more we are forced to admit our failure with shame.

It is not that ignorance, poverty, or weakness are good in themselves — they are not. The point is that we must recognize ourselves as such before God: poor, weak, and in need. In Him, not in ourselves, we find the true wealth, wisdom, and strength to transform the world.

Let us therefore ask God today to make us worthy of being called blessed: recognizing ourselves as poor in spirit; as those who continually need God’s light; ready to endure persecution for justice; and finally — so that we may bring peace to the world as Jesus teaches, and thus truly deserve to be called children of God.
